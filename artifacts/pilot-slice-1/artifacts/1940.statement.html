Problem Statement

The method sumOfDigitsOfStringAfterConvert receives two parameters: a string s consisting of lowercase English letters, and an integer k indicating the number of digit‑summing rounds.

First, convert each letter of s to its position in the alphabet (a=1, b=2, …, z=26). Concatenate these decimal positions to form a single integer. For example, “abz” becomes “1 2 26” → “1226”.

Then perform exactly k rounds. In each round replace the current integer by the sum of its decimal digits. Rounds continue even if the number is already a single digit; a further round leaves it unchanged.

Return the integer obtained after the final round.

Constraints

  • 1≤|s|≤100
  • 1≤k≤10

Edge Cases

  • The digit‑summing rounds are executed exactly k times, no more, no less.
  • Letters whose alphabet position has two digits (10‑26) contribute two digits to the concatenated number.
  • If the number becomes a single digit before all rounds are finished, subsequent rounds leave it unchanged.

Worked Example

s = "abc"
k = 2
Conversion: a→1, b→2, c→3 → "123" (integer 123)
Round 1: 1+2+3 = 6
Round 2: 6 (single digit remains 6)
Result: 6
