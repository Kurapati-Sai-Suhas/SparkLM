You are given two strings, word1 and word2, and your task is to decide whether they use every letter of the alphabet at similar frequencies. Both word1 and word2 are strings of lowercase English letters and have the same length. Their length will be between one and 100 characters.

To determine if the strings are almost equivalent, for each of the twenty-six lowercase letters, you must count how many times it occurs in word1 and how many times it occurs in word2. Then, take the size of the difference between those two counts. The strings qualify as almost equivalent when every one of those differences is at most three.

Your method should output true if every letter's frequency difference is at most three, and false as soon as any letter exceeds it. It is crucial to note that every letter of the alphabet is checked, not only the letters that appear in the input strings. If a letter appears in neither string, its difference is zero. If a letter appears in only one string, its count is compared against a count of zero in the other. The threshold is at most three, meaning a difference of exactly three is still acceptable, while four fails. The difference is a size and is never negative.
